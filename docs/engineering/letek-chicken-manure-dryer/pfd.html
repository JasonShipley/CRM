Drum loading against MCE precedent. The XD-36 (3×20, 141 ft³) was sized for this customer at 865 lb/hr water, or 6.1 lb/hr·ft³. The XD-60 (5×30, 589 ft³) at 75 MT/day and 16 h was 4.1. The Phibro XD-96 Mk.1 carries a 30 MMBtu/hr burner, which is 17,100 lb/hr of water or 8.5 lb/hr·ft³ at full fire. Case A runs the same drum at 2.1, so the drum has room for case B and for the no-press fallback without changing the shell. What changes between cases is the burner, the exhaust train and the fan, not the drum.

What the drum size buys. A 6 ft drum (XD-72 class) would need 6–8 lb/hr·ft³ on a sticky 70% cake to hold case B, which is above anything MCE has run on manure. The 8×40 is not oversized for this feed; it is sized for the uncertainty in the press. If the press is later dropped, the XD-96 takes the 30 MMBtu/hr burner assembly already drawn for it.

Open items that change the answer ranked by how much of the duty they move
  1. Press cake moisture. 70% vs 75% MC is a 31% swing in dryer duty and the difference between a 10 and a 15 MMBtu/hr burner. Send the 5-gal sample (already requested of Bernardo in April) to a press vendor for a bench test before the number is firmed up.
  2. Solids capture and pressate disposition. The mass balance assumes every solid reaches the dryer. On real digestate a screw press captures 60–80% without polymer. The rest goes back to the digester or lagoon in 70 MT/day of pressate. LETEK is selling a treatment service, so they need to own that stream; confirm they have somewhere to put it.
  3. Ammonia in the exhaust. Digestate at pH 8 sheds ammonia as soon as it is heated. Odor and emissions on a poultry site outside Mérida will likely need a scrubber; it is drawn as optional and not in the quote. Ask LETEK for the emissions permit conditions.
  4. Biogas composition and pressure. H₂S sets the conditioning skid size and whether the gas train and burner survive; supply pressure sets the blower. The allowance in the quote stands until an analysis arrives.
  5. Operating hours. 24 h/day assumed because digester and effluent are continuous. At 20 h the duty and the exhaust train grow 20%.
  6. Effluent total solids. 15% TS is stated by the customer. If the true digestate is 10% TS the press and tank double in size while the dryer duty stays the same.
  7. Product cooling and storage. Dried manure at 180°F self-heats in a pile. Cooling conveyor and a first-in-first-out storage practice are needed; not a dryer problem but it will be MCE's problem if it catches fire.
Assumptions and confidence

Inputs used. Customer statements of 20 Aug 2026: 120–160 MT/day digestate at 85% MC, target 15% MC, biogas 7,455 Nm³/day at 55% CH₄ (145.77 MMBtu/day), pH 7.2–8.2 from two samples. MCE energy basis of 1,750 Btu/lb water from Jason's March calculation. XD-96 drum geometry and 30 MMBtu/hr burner from the XD-96 Mk.1 operating manual. Fuel price US$14.50/MMBtu from the March correspondence.

Assumed. Screw press cake 30% TS; 100% solids capture; 24 h/day; 1,100°F drum inlet and 230°F exhaust; 10% air in-leakage; cake bulk density 55 lb/ft³.

Confidence. High on the dryer platform and the thermal duty for a given cake moisture; the arithmetic is the same MCE used for the XD-36 and XD-60 quotes and reproduces the figures in the draft reply to Bernardo (7.3 and 18.5 MMBtu/hr, 83% biogas coverage, US$420 and US$2,500/day LPG). Medium on the exhaust volume and cyclone size, which are first-principles estimates pending MCE's calculator. Low on anything downstream of the press until a sample is tested. No prices or lead times are stated here; those belong to the quote.
