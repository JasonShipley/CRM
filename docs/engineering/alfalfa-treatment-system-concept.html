Alfalfa Treatment System

Two-Stage Sealed Trommel · Concept & Budgetary Estimate
MCE-ENG-2026-041  ·  Rev 0  ·  2026-08-24  ·  Budgetary — not for construction

Summary

A two-stage, fully sealed trommel system for chemical treatment of alfalfa, scaled to roughly half the linear size of our 105200100 cooler drum. Stage 1 is an indirectly fired 304SS trommel with methyltrichlorosilane (MTCS) injection. Stage 2 applies anhydrous ammonia through an internal spray bar. Three gas-tight rotary airlocks isolate the two chemical atmospheres from each other and from the room.

System Concept

Process schematic — stacked arrangement, gravity transfer. Each stage holds slight negative pressure and vents to its own scrubber; the two atmospheres never mix.

Stage 1 — Heated MTCS Trommel

  • Drum: Ø54″ × 12′, 10 GA 304SS shell with 304SS flights and tumbling plates, chain-ring drive on trunnion rollers — same architecture as 105200100 at half scale.
  • Indirect firing: the drum mid-section runs inside a stationary, insulated firing shroud. Two modulating burners (≈750 MBH each, 1.5 MMBtu/h total) fire into the annulus; flue gas never contacts the product. Refractory-lined floor, flue collar and stack connection on the shroud.
  • Sealing: stationary end breechings with dual floating face seals at each drum end, N₂-purged interspace, and a silicone/PTFE O-ring land following the 105200211 detail. The vent fan holds the drum interior at roughly −0.5″ WC so any seal leakage is inward.
  • MTCS injection: metering pump skid, day-tank containment, and a 316SS/PTFE lance with atomizing nozzles entering through the feed breeching, dry-N₂ carrier and purge. MTCS hydrolyzes on the moist alfalfa surface and liberates HCl — which is why the drum is sealed, ventilated, and scrubbed.

Stage 2 — Ammonia Trommel

  • Drum: identical Ø54″ × 12′ 304SS trommel, unheated, same seal system.
  • NH₃ application: full-length internal spray bar fed from a vaporizer/regulation train, metered against feed rate. All wetted trim steel or stainless — no copper, brass, or zinc anywhere in the ammonia circuit.
  • Vent: dedicated water scrubber; ammonia detection at the seals and in the room.

Isolation & Airlocks

Three gas-tight rotary airlocks — feed, interstage transfer, and discharge — in 304SS with reduced-clearance rotors and purge ports. The interstage transfer chute is N₂-purged and monitored.

The two chemistries must never meet. MTCS vapor and its HCl by-product react violently with ammonia and generate dense ammonium-chloride fume. The interstage airlock plus purged transfer section is not optional — it is the control that keeps Stage 1 and Stage 2 atmospheres separate. Vent streams stay segregated through their own scrubbers and combine only after scrubbing.

Material caution, stated plainly: 304SS is specified per your direction and is priced that way. Wet HCl attacks 304 by chloride pitting. For the Stage 1 spray zone and vapor path, 316L internals are a cheap insurance policy — priced as an option below. A HAZOP before detail design is strongly recommended; MTCS is highly flammable and water-reactive, so spray-zone electrical is treated as classified area equipment.

Assumptions & Confidence

Inputs used: 105200100 drawing package and BOM (Rev A, 5/26/25) as the scale and architecture basis; your direction on 304SS, indirect firing, MTCS, NH₃ and sealing.

Assumed, not confirmed: geometric half-scale (≈1/8 the reference swept volume) is acceptable for the duty; product temperature ≤300 °F; continuous operation; chemicals owner-supplied at the skid edge; non-classified room with classified zones local to the spray points.

Confidence: HIGH that the architecture is buildable on the 105200100 platform. LOW–MEDIUM on price at this stage — the seal system, scrubbers, and burner package carry the widest spread.

What firms this up

  • Required throughput (lb/hr) and residence time per stage — this sets final drum size, and may move it off exact half scale
  • Treatment temperature and moisture in/out for Stage 1
  • MTCS and NH₃ dose rates per ton — sizes the injection skids and scrubbers
  • Batch vs. continuous, and the emission limit the scrubbers must meet
  • Site electrical classification and the 316L decision
