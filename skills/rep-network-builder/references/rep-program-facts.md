# MCE Rep Program — What Already Exists

Recovered from MCE's own Gmail history, Feb–Aug 2026. This corrects a wrong assumption: the rep
program is **not greenfield**. There is a real contract, a real process, real signed/in-flight reps,
and international sales already happening. Recruiting CPM's reps is an *expansion* of a running
program, not the founding of one.

Re-verify the roster before every outreach pass — this file is a snapshot, not live state.

## The contract

**Template:** `MCE Exclusive Sales Representative Agreement - TEMPLATE.pdf` — the file Jason sends
prospective reps as "our standard contract." Note **Exclusive** in the title: exclusive territory is
the model MCE already offers, not an open question.

Structure, per Ron Dominguez (Feb 26, 2026): *"Exhibit A is now the Territory, you can change per each
different Rep Company, keep the contract the same. Exhibit B is House Accounts.... Both can change
without"* touching the body of the agreement.

- **Exhibit A — Territory.** Per-rep. This is the negotiated field. With WD Patterson the negotiation
  was over how to split Missouri, so territory can be carved sub-state, not just state-by-state.
- **Exhibit B — House Accounts.** Named accounts MCE retains. **InstaPro** was named as a house
  account (Feb 26, 2026), with Jason noting the designation may change if MCE acquires them.
- **Body — fixed.** Do not renegotiate the body per rep; that's the whole point of the exhibit design.

**Commission.** Jason Bliss's position, Feb 25, 2026: *"I feel we only give house accounts a 7.5%."*
So the structure is tiered — a standard rate on rep-developed accounts and roughly **7.5% on house
accounts**. The standard rate lives in the PDF and is **not confirmed in the email record**; get it
from Ron or the template itself before quoting any number to a rep. **Clause 5.4** was the passage
JB expected reps to push back on (*"I am not sure how they are going to take 5.4"*) — read it before
any commission conversation so you know what the objection will be.

**Insurance.** Reps carry general liability, reduced from $2M to **$1M** during the WD Patterson
negotiation (Mar 2–3, 2026). That's a real barrier for a small rep firm and worth knowing early.

**Approval gate.** Jason Shipley, Feb 25, 2026: *"we need his blessing before we send anything."*
Nothing goes to a rep without Jason's sign-off — consistent with this skill's hard boundary.

## What this changes about recruiting CPM's reps

- **Don't ask Jason for terms he already has.** Territory exclusivity, house accounts, the tiered
  commission, and the insurance requirement are all settled in the template. Pull terms from the
  agreement, don't invent them.
- **Exclusivity makes territory conflicts real, not theoretical.** Every new rep's Exhibit A has to be
  drawn against territory already granted. Check the existing roster before promising any geography —
  granting overlapping exclusive territory is a contract problem, not a sales problem.
- **The direct-call team is the other collision.** Paula, Ron, Magda, and Jason call mills directly. In
  an exclusive-territory model, a mill inside a rep's Exhibit A that the direct team is also calling is
  either a house account under Exhibit B or it's the rep's. Resolve it on paper, per account, before it
  becomes an argument over a commission check.
- **Jason Bliss is the channel, in both directions.** He is the insider source for identifying CPM's
  unhappy reps *and* the person who already owns rep relationships. Route through him rather than
  around him.
