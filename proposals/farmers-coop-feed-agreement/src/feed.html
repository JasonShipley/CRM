This Front-End Engineering and Design (FEED) Agreement covers the engineering needed to take Farmers Cooperative's new tub plant and feed mill from budgetary pricing to a firm, buildable design and price. The work is in three parts. Each part can be authorized on its own, and each design FEED is credited in full toward its plant when that plant is ordered.

1. Part A — Site Scan and 3D Site Model $17,950 · 3–4 weeks

MCE visits the selected Van Buren site and captures it with a 3D laser scanner. From the scan, MCE builds a rough 3D model of the site, places the tub plant and feed mill in it, and lays out how trucks and rail cars move through the site. The model gives Farmers Cooperative and its board a clear picture of the whole project and is the base for the Part B and Part C layouts.

Scope

  • One site trip by MCE (about 1–2 days on site), travel included
  • 3D laser scan of the selected site: existing buildings, grades, roads, rail, tanks and visible utilities
  • Registration of the point cloud and a rough 3D model of the existing site
  • Block placement of the tub plant building, feed mill, bulk storage bins, flat storage, bulk loadout and the molasses rail unloading and tank
  • Truck flow (receiving, scale, loadout, bagged product) and rail flow for molasses
  • Review meeting with Farmers Cooperative (online) and one revision

Deliverables

  • Registered point cloud of the site (common file format)
  • 3D site model with both plants placed, and a viewer file for the board
  • Site plan drawing with building placement and truck and rail traffic flow
  • Short site memo: utilities seen, access and constraints, and items for the civil engineer

Additional site

If Farmers Cooperative wants a second candidate site scanned and modeled on the same trip, it is added at $9,950 per site.

2. Part B — Tub Plant Design FEED $69,950 · 6–8 weeks

Part B takes the 4 TPH cooked tub plant in proposal MCE-Q-2610-FCO-R1 to a final design and a firm price, placed in the Part A site model.

Scope

  • Final tub formulas with an independent ruminant nutritionist, coordinated by MCE
  • Design basis: rate, products, tub sizes, operating schedule, annual and peak volume
  • Process flow diagram with mass and heat balance
  • Plant and building layout in the site model, including the cure tunnel length from the cure time
  • Dry ingredient handling: station and micro bin count, and weighing method from the final formulas
  • Molasses receiving and storage, including rail unloading if used
  • Heat source selection and sizing; shared steam with the feed mill if Part C is also authorized
  • Equipment list and specifications; vendor quotes on the major bought-out items
  • Electrical load list, MCC and controls architecture

Deliverables

  • Design basis and final formulas
  • Process flow diagram with mass and heat balance
  • General arrangement drawings: plan and elevations
  • Equipment list and specifications
  • Utility requirements: power, gas, steam or heat, water and air
  • Foundation and equipment loads for the civil engineer
  • Project schedule
  • Firm price for the plant, replacing the budgetary price in MCE-Q-2610-FCO-R1

3. Part C — Feed Mill Design FEED $46,950 · 6–8 weeks

Part C designs the new Van Buren feed mill that replaces the Sallisaw mill: receiving, bulk storage, grinding, batching with micro ingredients, mixing, a sweet feed line, pelleting, cooling, screening, bulk loadout, and bagging with robotic palletizing. Design basis is 20,000–25,000 tons per year at a pellet rate of 7–10 TPH. The mill is placed in the Part A site model.

Scope

  • Design basis: capacities, products and forms, ingredient list, storage days at peak, operating schedule
  • Process flow diagram with mass and steam balance
  • Mill layout in the site model: receiving, bins, grinding, batching, pelleting, cooling and screening, loadout and bagging
  • Equipment list and specifications; vendor quotes on the major packages
  • Structural concept for the bins, batching structure and loadout structure, with foundation loads for the civil engineer
  • Electrical and controls concept: motor list, MCC, batching system and I/O count
  • Steam generator sizing, including whether an existing 40 BHP Clayton unit is suitable
  • Preliminary dust hazard review per NFPA 61 and 652

Deliverables

  • Design basis
  • Process flow diagram with mass and steam balance
  • General arrangement drawings: plan and elevations
  • Equipment list and specifications
  • Structural concept and foundation loads
  • Electrical and controls concept; utility requirements
  • Preliminary dust hazard review memo
  • Project schedule
  • Firm price for the mill, replacing the budgetary price
